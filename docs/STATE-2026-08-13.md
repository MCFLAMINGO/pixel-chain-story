## The requirement everything else is ranked against

> "I need to be able to walk away and it works."

Today it doesn't. One Railway volume holds the only copy of the history **and** the
only key that can extend it. If the bill goes unpaid the chain is gone — the
anchors would prove a picture existed and what its digest was, and nobody could
reconstruct a single pixel.

This reorders the whole roadmap. Emission mechanics are the interesting design and
they are refinements of a thing that currently evaporates.

**Priority order:**

1. **Copy the node datadir to one other person.** No code. Chain plus identity.
   Takes the worst case from _gone_ to _someone else presses go_.
2. **Deploy the site.** Merged wallet work is not in the bundle, so five people
   cannot export their wallets.
3. **One or two witnesses.** Three commands each; independent copies.
4. **Anchor key** — see below.
5. Everything else.

## Decisions — settled, do not relitigate

**Supply cap is 10,300,000,000** — one PIX per human alive at the projected peak of
humanity (~2084). Not Satoshi's 21,000,000, which meant nothing here. A cap needs
no oracle, which is why the census works as a ceiling where it failed as a peg.

**Emission is flat, not halving.** Halving front-loads a mining subsidy; there is no
hash race to subsidise. It also could not reach its own ceiling — inherited without
Bitcoin's base unit, it died 630,000 short.

**Gifts are free; records cost.** See [`GIFT-AND-RECORD.md`](./GIFT-AND-RECORD.md).
One gift per pair ever; a record costs three PIX from three distinct givers; light
is spent into the picture rather than burned; the witness recycles its share into
onboarding.

Four of those are now enforced rather than described — `gift-and-record.ts` refuses
the gift cap, the pair limit, the quorum and the picture's share, and the selftest
shows a bad moment cannot reach a block. Provenance, which the quorum and the pair
limit both need, is read from history in `provenance.ts`, so a restart cannot forget
what a counter in node memory would have. **The policy is off unless a network sets
`PIXEL_GIFT_AND_RECORD=1`**: these are consensus rules and the crowned Earth has
real people on it, so turning them on there is a ceremony, not a deploy.

Still prose: the witness recycling its share (behaviour, not consensus — a witness
could sit on it) and where the picture's colour comes from.

**Open, and blocking:** the gift mint-back is not implemented and must not be until
identity costs something. Full reasoning, with the arithmetic and three claims I got
wrong along the way, in **[`FARMING-AND-THE-MINT-BACK.md`](./FARMING-AND-THE-MINT-BACK.md)**.
The short form:

- A fresh address is always a new pair, so the pair limit cannot object to one. Case 7
  of the economy-model selftest turns 0 PIX and 2000 free addresses into 2000 PIX and
  1000 records, and is pinned as a passing test so closing it fails loudly.
- Per-pair budgets are quadratic — K devices command K(K−1) pairs — so a bigger farm
  pays _less_ per PIX. A per-identity budget is scale-invariant; at G = 1 the cap and
  the emission rule become one sentence. **Neither half works alone**: a budget over
  free addresses is free too.
- There is no forgery to detect. A room of devices genuinely _is_ present, and presence
  cannot be proven by the pair. Only a witness outside it prices anything (~$300/PIX
  tightly dialled, against $0.01 emulated).
- The harm is **not** exclusion and **not** capture of the record — both claims were
  wrong. A person can always be _given_ light, and a hoard writes nothing while writing
  liquidates the hoard. What survives is the on-ramp for people who have no light yet.
- The chain's conservation invariants (PIX-02/03) are what currently prevent all of it.

**The cap has a horizon.** At 132M births a year, 10.3e9 is 78 years of free welcomes
with zero farming: `PIX_HARD_CAP` is peak _simultaneous_ population, but welcomes
accumulate across generations (~117bn humans ever, eleven times the cap). It is a
subsidy with an end date, not a seat per person — fine once named, not fine while being
described as one PIX per human forever.

**No bridge.** The protocol never sells PIX. Peer trade needs no code and makes us
no party — Bitcoin's first year, and most of why Satoshi was not the operator of
anything. The bridge was also the single source of the custody, Ponzi and
money-transmitter exposure.

**Measure the dark, do not enforce it.** Demurrage was rejected: it cannot tell an
absent person from a farm standing still, so it charges both the same. Supply goes
quiet on its own when people do, and `litSupplyReport()` reads it.

**The crowned Earth cannot be made twice.** Lab chains use `PIXEL_LAB_NETWORK_ID`;
network 20553 accepts only `CROWNED_GENESIS_HASH`; the ceremony forges a separate
network and says so.

**Pixel is not Ethereum.** It does not need a validator set to resist capture by the
rich. It needs nobody to be able to quietly rewrite what happened — which anchors
plus witnesses already provide. What a single sequencer lacks is liveness and
censorship resistance, which is a **succession** problem, not a consensus one.

## Open questions

- **Is PIX purchasable at all?** Everything above says no. Nothing enforces it —
  peer sales cannot be prevented, only not built.
- Where the picture's colour comes from: an image contributes its own, a gift takes
  the pair's. The current green is authored by `spectrumToRgb`.
- Whether the palette is canonical or the viewer's choice.
- Who witnesses: earned by presence, constrained by capability, rotated by lottery —
  blocked on membership living in the chain.

## Prerequisites, in order

1. ~~**`bigint` divisibility.**~~ **Stale — not currently required.** Written for the
   earlier design (two or three PIX per pixel split among N participants, plus a
   tithe), which needed fractions. [`GIFT-AND-RECORD.md`](./GIFT-AND-RECORD.md)
   superseded it the same night and is integer-native: a gift is one PIX, a record
   is three, split one/one/one. Nothing in it needs a fraction.

   The facts, so this can be re-decided rather than re-discovered:
   - `PIX_BASE_UNITS` appears only in reporting (`emissionInfo`, `rewardUnits`,
     `totalBaseUnitsAtCap`). The money path never touches it.
   - The precision cliff is real but unreachable today — `cap × 1e8` is exact
     because it is a round number, while `cap × 1e8 + 1` silently loses the 1.
     Nothing computes at that scale while the ledger counts whole PIX.
   - Cost if attempted: ~375 occurrences across 87 files, and `JSON.stringify`
     throws on `bigint` — so the persisted, gossiped and served wire format all
     change, on a live chain with real balances.

   **Do it when something actually needs a fraction** — a tithe, a split, or
   per-content pricing finer than whole PIX. Not before.

2. **A record-only moment.** Every moment is currently a transfer with a positive
   amount, so "Harry met Sally" cannot be written without paying someone.
3. **Sequencer membership in the chain.** `scripts/electable-drift-selftest.ts`
   reproduces the failure: membership is gossip-derived but `acceptBlock` demands an
   exact match, so two operators reject each other's blocks. Until then, more
   operators means more disagreement. Joining defaults to witness for this reason.

## The recurring bug

Four times in two days: a failure rendering as an ordinary state.

- The anchor job went green having published nothing.
- Verify reported agreement as divergence, and an idle chain as broken.
- The wallet showed a balance for a chain it could not reach.
- Storage said "no wallet" when it meant "I could not look", inviting a user to
  forge a second identity over their first.

All fixed. **The pattern is worth watching for** — optimistic paths and swallowed
errors. A failure that looks normal is worse than an error, because the user acts
on it.

## Things that are true and easy to forget

- The whole chain is ~1.4 MB. Every wallet could hold a copy —
  [`chain-mirror.ts`](../src/lib/pixel/chain-mirror.ts).
- The export can be a **self-rendering HTML file**: pixels plus the code to draw
  them, no external reference, opens offline in any browser.
- Anchoring proves publication time, not correctness. Catching a false-but-immutable
  root needs an independent archive — witnesses are that archive.
- `assertCrownedEarth` had zero call sites for months. Enforcement functions that
  are never called look exactly like enforcement.
- CI never ran `tsc`. A ratchet now holds the ceiling at 22 pre-existing errors.
- Manual testing found two defects in the export work that reading the diff did not.
  UI changes need a browser, not a review.
